## Voice

- **Plain and direct.** “Enter the contact’s name.”, not “Contact name is a mandatory field.”
- **Specific.** “4 items will be removed from the order”, not “This action will affect your data.”
- **Calm.** No exclamation marks, no blame and no jokes in errors.
- **People-first.** Talk about the person’s task (“Save the document”), not the system (“Persist record”).
- **Consistent.** One name per thing across a product. Don’t call the same thing an order, a request and a ticket on different screens.

## Capitalisation

| Element                                                         | Style                             | Example                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Buttons, menu items, action tooltips, segment and tab titles    | Title Case                        | Save Document, Apply Filter…, Add Item |
| Page and sheet titles                                           | Title Case                        | Team Members, Add Contact              |
| Alert titles                                                    | Title Case question or statement  | Delete This Project?                   |
| Labels, descriptions, messages, toasts, empty states            | Sentence case                     | Email address · No saved drafts        |
| Column headings                                                 | Sentence case, short nouns        | Name · Qty · Amount                    |

In Title Case, capitalise every word except articles, short conjunctions and short prepositions: “Save and Close”, “Add to Project”.

## Buttons and menu items

- A verb, or a verb and a noun: Save, Add Contact, Duplicate.
- Add an ellipsis (…) when the control needs more input before acting, because it opens a sheet, a menu of options or a file chooser: Print…, Export…. No ellipsis when it acts immediately.
- No articles (“Add Item”, not “Add an Item”), no “please”, no “click here”.
- Paired choices say what each does: Cancel / Delete Project, Skip / Continue. Never OK / Cancel for a decision.

## Messages

**Errors** say what happened and what to do, in one or two sentences:

- “Printer not connected. Check the cable and try again.”
- “Couldn’t save your changes. Check your connection, then save again.”
- Field errors are instructions: “Enter a price above zero.”

**Confirmations** state the consequence: “Its 4 tasks will be deleted too. You can’t undo this.”

**Toasts** confirm in a few words: “Document saved”, “3 people invited”, “Item removed”. Fragments don’t need a full stop.

**Empty states** name what goes here and how to add it: “No saved drafts. Drafts you save appear here.”

## Labels and placeholders

- Labels are short nouns above the field: “Mobile number”, “Due date”.
- Placeholders show an example or a format, never the label: “e.g. Asha Patil”.
- Help text under a field explains consequences or rules: “Shown on invoices and statements.”
- Mark the exception: add “(optional)” to the few optional fields instead of marking every required one.

## Numbers, money, dates and times

- **Money** follows the reader’s locale for symbol, grouping and decimals, with two decimals in tables and totals. In prose, round when exactness doesn’t matter.
- **Quantities** put the unit beside the number (“2 kg”, “12 pcs”), with tabular figures in columns.
- **Dates** follow the locale in fields and tables. In headings and sentences, write them out (“2 October 2026”) and use “Today” and “Yesterday” where it helps.
- **Times** follow the device’s 12- or 24-hour clock.
- **Counts** use numerals and agree in number: “1 item”, “4 items”, “No items”.
- **Signs** use “+12” and “−4” (a true minus) when change matters; colour is never the only signal.

## Words for access

- Every icon-only control has an accessible label, with the same words as its tooltip.
- Links and buttons make sense out of context: “Open Settings”, not “Click here”.
- Avoid directional words alone (“the button on the right”) and colour alone (“the red items”).
